\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluated whether reward-model verifiers in Best-of-$N$ mathematical answer selection
can be quantized without losing selection accuracy or their advantage over majority
vote. On frozen candidate pools and with analysis plans fixed in advance, the tested
4-bit GPTQ deployments of verifiers with 4B or more parameters kept Best-of-32 accuracy
within a $\pm3$\pp\ equivalence margin in the main study and in two further studies (H6 and H7).
Where the verifier beat majority vote, the quantized verifier kept more than half of
that advantage, an estimated 93\% for the PRM. Under the tested 6~GiB verifier budget,
the 4B ORM with packed 4-bit weights was the best tested deployment. Quantization often
changes the selected candidate but changes the selected answer much less often, which is
consistent with answer redundancy in the pools. Three-bit GPTQ with generic calibration
degrades selection, most for the smallest Qwen-based ORM and severely for both
Llama-based reward models. Calibrating on in-domain candidates recovers most of the
loss for both verifiers that failed among those we re-quantized (the 1.7B ORM and
Tulu-3), and a length-matched generic control does not.

These results carry two practical lessons. First, a verifier should be compared with
majority vote on the target generator, because with a strong generator it may add
little. Second, selection should be evaluated with a grader that is robust to answer
format, because a strict grader can turn format preferences into spurious effects of
quantization.
