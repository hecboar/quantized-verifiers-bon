\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether the reward-model verifier in Best-of-$N$ mathematical answer selection
can be quantized without losing what it is for. On frozen candidate pools, with
prospectively specified analysis plans, the tested 4-bit GPTQ deployments of verifiers
with 4B parameters and above kept Best-of-32 accuracy within a $\pm3$\pp\ equivalence
margin in the main study and in two follow-up studies, and where the verifier beat
majority vote they kept more than half of that advantage (an estimated 93\% for the
PRM). Under the tested 6~GiB verifier budget, the 4B ORM with packed 4-bit weights was
the best tested deployment. Quantization changes the selected candidate often, but the
selected answer much less often, consistent with answer redundancy in the pools. Three-bit
GPTQ with generic calibration degrades selection: among the Qwen-based ORMs most for the
smallest one, and severely for both Llama-based reward models; for the Qwen-based ORMs,
in-domain calibration recovers most of the loss.
Two practical caveats travel with these results. Compare a verifier against majority
vote on one's own generator, because with a strong generator it may add little; and
evaluate selection with a grader that is robust to answer format, because a strict
grader can turn format preferences into spurious effects of quantization.
