\section{Experimental setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Analysis plans}\label{sec:plans}

The design, estimands and tests were specified prospectively in a version-controlled
repository, with a stated chronology (Table~\ref{tab:chronology} in
\ref{app:deviations}). For the main study the plan and its amendments were committed
after the candidate pools had been generated but before any verifier score on the
evaluation problems existed; only generator-side statistics (accuracy, share of
extractable answers, truncation) had been inspected at that point. The two follow-up
studies were fixed, with their problem manifests and analysis code, before their
candidates were generated. We did not use an external registry, so the commit history
is the record of what was fixed when. Hypotheses are labelled c1--c5 (main study),
H6a/b and H7a/b (follow-up studies); results not covered by these tests are
exploratory and labelled as such.

\subsection{Problems and candidate pools}\label{sec:pools}

Table~\ref{tab:populations} lists every population. The main evaluation set has 2,000
problems: 800 from the GSM8K test split \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k}, 400 sampled from MATH500
\cite{lightman2023verify}, and 800 further MATH test problems \cite{hendrycks2021math}
outside MATH500, sampled with a fixed seed. The first 1,200 (GSM8K and MATH500) form the
original manifest, frozen before the MATH extension was added, and are the registered
population of c1. A development set of 400 problems (200 GSM8K train, 200 MATH train) was
used only for the go/no-go gate and for choices assigned to development data.

The main generator is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct \cite{qwen2.5}. For every problem it
produces a pool of 32 candidates (4 seeds $\times$ 8 samples, temperature 0.8,
top-$p$ 1.0, at most 1,024 new tokens) with the system prompt ``Please reason step by
step, and put your final answer within \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}.'' Pools are
generated once in BF16 with vLLM 0.30 \cite{kwon2023vllm}, frozen and hashed; every
verifier and precision scores exactly the same candidates. Candidate accuracy is
52.1\%, 88.7\% of candidates have an extractable final answer, and 6.5\% reach the
length limit. A secondary generator, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, produces pools for a
300-problem subset (exploratory; Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).

\subsection{Verifiers}\label{sec:verifiers}

The main study uses three outcome reward models (ORMs) of one family,
Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3 at 1.7B, 4B and 8B parameters \cite{skywork2025v2}, and one
process reward model (PRM), Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B \cite{zhang2025prm}. The Skywork models
are general preference reward models, trained on broad human-preference data rather
than on mathematical correctness; we use them as outcome verifiers by scoring the full
(problem, response) pair with their scalar head. The PRM is trained for mathematical
step correctness; its step rewards are aggregated by their minimum (the last-step
reward is reported as a sensitivity analysis). To test generality beyond this family,
two further ORMs on a different backbone are evaluated in exploratory experiments:
Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B (same training-data family, Llama~3.1 backbone) and
Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B-RM \cite{lambert2024tulu3} (different laboratory and data). All
models are run with their documented input formats (\ref{app:impl}).

\subsection{Precisions and deployments}\label{sec:precisions}

Quantization is weight-only: the weights of all linear layers inside the transformer
blocks are quantized, while embeddings, norms and the reward head stay in BF16, and
activations and computation stay in BF16. ``BF16'' denotes the uncompressed model. The
conditions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NF4}: 4-bit NormalFloat \cite{dettmers2023qlora}, both simulated and
deployed with \texttt{bitsandbytes} (bit-identical scores);
\item \textbf{GPTQ-W4} and \textbf{GPTQ-W3}: GPTQ \cite{frantar2023gptq} with group
size 128, calibrated on 128 WikiText-2 sequences of 512 tokens (generic calibration);
\item \textbf{AWQ-W4} \cite{lin2024awq} (same calibration) and round-to-nearest
\textbf{RTN-W4}/\textbf{RTN-W8} (exploratory dose-response).
\end{itemize}
Accuracy experiments use simulated (``fake'') quantization: weights are quantized and
dequantized to BF16, so only the rounding changes. For the memory study, GPTQ-W4 models
are exported and run with packed 4-bit kernels (\texttt{gptqmodel}, Marlin). Every
quantized model is audited: all target layers must change and no other parameter may
change.

Verifier memory is the peak allocated GPU memory, in GiB ($2^{30}$ bytes), while
scoring a fixed workload of 64 sequences of 900 tokens on an RTX 4090 with no generator
resident.

\subsection{Selection rules and baselines}\label{sec:selection}

Best-of-$N$ selects the highest-scoring candidate among $N$; exact score ties are broken
by a fixed random priority (seed 11) shared by all conditions. For $N<32$, accuracy is
averaged over 200 random subsets per problem, drawn identically for every condition
(seed 7); $N=32$ is the full pool. Two verifier-free baselines are reported. Majority
vote is a plurality vote over canonical answer clusters (numeric normalisation, then
symbolic equivalence with \texttt{math\_verify}): candidates without an extractable
answer do not vote, the most frequent cluster wins, ties go to the tied cluster that
appears first in candidate order, a pool without any extractable answer counts as
incorrect, and the vote is correct if its cluster contains a correct candidate. It does
not depend on the verifier. The oracle (pass@32) is correct if any candidate is.

\subsection{Grading}\label{sec:grading}

The registered grader extracts the last \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}} answer (else a
GSM8K-style ``\#\#\#\#'' line or an explicit ``the answer is'' phrase), normalises
numbers and compares symbolically for MATH. Because a strict grader can confound
verifier preferences with answer format, we report three checks: a lenient grader (the
last number or expression in the final lines when no answer is extractable; sensitivity
only), a blinded human audit (\ref{app:audit}), and a re-grading of GSM8K with the
revised GSM8K-Platinum labels \cite{vendrow2025platinum}. The endpoint throughout is
final-answer correctness.

\subsection{Metrics and controls}\label{sec:metrics}

For verifier $v$ and condition $c$, $A_N$ is Best-of-$N$ accuracy and
$\Delta_N = A_N^{c} - A_N^{\mathrm{BF16}}$, computed per problem and averaged. At
$N=32$ we also report answer transitions relative to BF16: the share of problems whose
selected candidate changes, whose selected canonical answer changes, whose correct BF16
selection becomes wrong (harm) and the reverse (benefit), so that $\Delta_{32}$ equals
benefit minus harm. Two controls characterise numerical sensitivity: \emph{bf16-null}
rescores in BF16 with a different batch layout, and \emph{bf16-repeat} rescores with the
same layout. The latter is bit-identical; the former changes the selected candidate on
7--11\% of main-study problems with $|\Delta_{32}| \le 0.3$\pp.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:stats}

All inference uses a paired percentile bootstrap over problems with 10,000 resamples
(seed 7). Problems are resampled with replacement from the full population, and every
quantity computed on a problem (both conditions, all verifiers entering a contrast, the
majority-vote outcome) is resampled jointly, so within-problem correlations are kept.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Differences} are reported with 95\% percentile intervals and two-sided
centred-bootstrap $p$-values.
\item \emph{Equivalence} at the registered $\pm3$\pp\ margin is tested by two one-sided
tests (TOST): $p_{\mathrm{TOST}} = \max\{P^*(\bar d \le -0.03),\, P^*(\bar d \ge 0.03)\}$,
which inverts the percentile interval ($p_{\mathrm{TOST}} \le \alpha$ if and only if the
$(1-2\alpha)$ interval lies inside the margin). In a simulation at the margin
($n=800$, skewed three-valued per-problem differences, 2,000 replicates) the rejection
rate was 0.056, 0.024 and 0.006 at nominal 0.05, 0.025 and 0.0125 (Monte Carlo standard
error about 0.005).
\item \emph{Value retention} (H6b, H7b) is one-sided: $p = P^*(\bar R \le 0)$
(Section~\ref{sec:h6}).
\item \emph{Resolution}: when no resample exceeds the boundary we report
$p \le 10^{-4}$ (and $\le m \cdot 10^{-4}$ after Holm adjustment of the $m$-th smallest
$p$-value), never $p=0$.
\item \emph{Multiplicity}: Holm correction within each prespecified family (c1--c5;
H6a/b; H7a/b); a test of the family that was not run keeps $p=1$. The registered
decision rule for c1 is that its 90\% interval lies inside $\pm3$\pp.
\end{itemize}
\noindent The registered contrasts are: c1, $\Delta_{32}$ of the 8B ORM at GPTQ-W4 on
the original 1,200-problem manifest (equivalence); c2, the development-selected
deployment versus the best BF16 deployment under the 6~GB verifier memory ceiling; c3, a
calibration method that was not run; c4, $\Delta_{32}$ on the 1,200 MATH problems (4B and
8B ORMs, GPTQ-W4, averaged within problem); c5, the change of that loss from $N=4$ to
$N=32$. Table~\ref{tab:confirmatory} summarises every confirmatory test.
