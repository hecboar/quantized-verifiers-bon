\section{Implementation details}\label{app:impl}

\paragraph{Prompt} Every generator receives the system prompt ``Please reason step by
step, and put your final answer within \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}.'' and the
problem as the user turn, through its chat template. For Llama-3.2-3B the instruction is
repeated at the end of the user turn (recorded deviation; without it 34\% of its
candidates had no extractable answer).

\paragraph{Verifier inputs} The ORMs (Skywork-Reward-V2-Qwen3 1.7B/4B/8B,
Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.1-Tulu-3-8B-RM) receive the (problem, response)
pair as a user/assistant conversation formatted by their chat template, without system
prompt, with a single beginning-of-sequence token, and output a scalar from their reward
head; the padding token never occurs inside a sequence (checked). Tulu-3-8B-RM does not
define a padding id in its configuration, so the tokenizer's padding token is set. The
PRM receives its documented system prompt, the problem, and the response split into
steps at blank lines, separated by its step token; the score is the minimum step reward
and the last-step reward is stored for sensitivity analysis.

\paragraph{Quantization} All linear layers inside the transformer blocks are quantized
(weight-only); embeddings, norms and the reward head stay in BF16, and activations stay
in BF16. GPTQ uses group size 128, symmetric quantization and 1\% diagonal damping,
calibrated on 128 WikiText-2 sequences of 512 tokens drawn with a fixed seed. AWQ uses
the same calibration and group size; NF4 uses \texttt{bitsandbytes} without double
quantization; RTN uses group size 128 at 4 bits and per-channel scales at 8 bits. Every
quantized model is audited by fingerprinting all parameters before and after
quantization: every target layer must change (e.g.\ 252 of 252 for the 8B ORM, 196 of
196 for the PRM) and no other parameter may change.

\paragraph{PRM rotary buffers}\label{app:prm} With \texttt{transformers} 5.x,
Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B's remote modelling code is instantiated on the meta device and its
non-persistent rotary-embedding buffers come back uninitialised after loading. Scores
computed this way were below the random-candidate rate. We recompute the buffers exactly
as the model's own initialisation does and check them at every load; all PRM results in
this paper use repaired buffers, and the affected scores were discarded (recorded
deviation).

\paragraph{Software and compute} Generation used vLLM 0.30.0; scoring used PyTorch
2.13.0 (CUDA 13.0) and \texttt{transformers} 5.17 (main study) or 5.19 (follow-up
studies); packed kernels used \texttt{gptqmodel}. Model identifiers and all settings are
recorded in the repository and in the metadata of every score file. All experiments ran on single cloud GPUs (mostly RTX 4090; some follow-up
runs on L40S and other GPUs with at least 24~GB, with the BF16 and quantized runs of a
verifier always on the same GPU), for roughly 90 GPU-hours in total including setup.

\section{Blinded audit of the outcome measure}\label{app:audit}

All 94 distinct Best-of-32 winners without an extractable final answer in either arm of
the prespecified memory-budget contrast (c2) (48 in the 4B GPTQ-W4 arm and 68 in the
1.7B BF16 arm, 22 shared), plus 30 random winners with one, were shown
to a human rater (one of the authors) with the problem, the reference answer and the full
response, rendered with mathematical typesetting, and nothing else. The endpoint was
final-answer correctness; responses that reached the length limit were marked as such
and checked individually, and by a rule fixed beforehand were counted as incorrect when
they stopped before stating a final answer. Of the 24 truncated responses audited, 23
were judged incorrect; one (R079) had stated its final answer before the cut and was
judged correct.
The rater judged 83 correct, 29 incorrect and 12 unsure. A blinded second pass (same view,
key unopened) re-examined every unsure and incorrect verdict, resolved all 12 unsure
verdicts as incorrect (11 cut by the length limit before giving a final answer, 3 of them
on a correct track, and one with a wrong final answer) and confirmed every incorrect one; it also flagged two GSM8K
problems with questionable labels, both of which GSM8K-Platinum removes. With the first
verdicts and unsure items dropped, the corrected c2 is $+1.41$\pp\ $[+0.35, +2.46]$
instead of $+1.40$. On the 30 controls the strict grader and the human agree on 29. On
the 94 winners without an extractable answer the strict grader scores all as wrong, the
human judges 60 correct (27 of 48 in the 4B arm, 45 of 68 in the 1.7B arm, 12 of
the 22 shared), and the lenient grader agrees with the human on 85. The audit
covers all unparseable winners plus 30 parseable controls and was done by a single rater; residual grading error
among parseable winners is not measured beyond the 30 controls.

\section{Analysis plans and deviations}\label{app:deviations}

Table~\ref{tab:chronology} lists the chronology recorded in the repository. The
deviations, all documented with dates, were: the Llama prompt repetition; the PRM
rotary-buffer repair and rescoring; the frozen calibration hyper-parameter of the unrun
c3 method (the prespecified decision rule permitted running c3, but at the prespecified
4 bits the 8B ORM showed no MATH damage to repair, so it was not run and stays in the
family with $p=1$); exploratory additions after the main
results (bit-width dose-response, the 1.7B packed model in the frontier); in H6, the loss
and regeneration of the first candidate pool (never inspected) and scoring split across
four GPUs; in H7, GPUs other than the RTX 4090 for lack of availability and lenient-grader
verdicts computed with time limits equivalent to those on Linux; corrections to the
follow-up analysis script made before H7 data existed, which reproduce all H6 results
exactly; and the exploratory revision experiments (additional reward models,
task-matched 3-bit calibration, memory and throughput re-measurement), fixed before
their launch. One analysis correction was made after the main results: the
exploratory majority-vote baseline of the main study had broken ties between answer
clusters by verifier score and fell back to the Best-of-$N$ winner when no candidate had
an extractable answer. It was replaced on 14 October (commit \texttt{8a69567}) by the
verifier-independent rule of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, which changes the majority outcome
on 12--14 of the 2,000 main-study problems (72.4--72.7\% to 72.1\%) and affects only
exploratory main-study tables and figures; the H6 and H7 analyses always used the
verifier-independent rule, so no confirmatory test, gain $G$ or retention statistic $R$
changes.
