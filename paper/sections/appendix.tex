\section{Implementation details}\label{app:impl}

\paragraph{Prompt.} Every generator receives the system prompt ``Please reason step by
step, and put your final answer within \texttt{\textbackslash boxed\{\}}.'' and the
problem as the user turn, through its chat template. For Llama-3.2-3B the instruction is
repeated at the end of the user turn (registered deviation; without it 34\% of its
candidates had no extractable answer).

\paragraph{Verifier inputs.} The Skywork ORMs receive the (problem, response) pair as a
user/assistant conversation without system prompt and output a scalar from their reward
head. The PRM receives its documented system prompt, the problem, and the response split
into steps at blank lines, separated by its step token; the score is the minimum step reward (the last-step reward is stored for
sensitivity).

\paragraph{Quantization.} All linear layers inside the transformer blocks are quantized;
embeddings, norms and the reward head stay in BF16. GPTQ uses group size 128, symmetric
quantization and 1\% diagonal damping, calibrated on 128 WikiText-2 sequences of 512
tokens with a fixed seed. Every quantized model is audited by fingerprinting all
parameters before and after quantization: every target layer must change (252 of 252
for the 8B ORM, 196 of 196 for the PRM) and no other parameter may change.

\paragraph{PRM rotary buffers.}\label{app:prm} Under recent versions of
\texttt{transformers}, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B's remote modelling code is instantiated on the
meta device and its non-persistent rotary-embedding buffers come back uninitialised
after loading. Scores computed this way were below the random-candidate rate. We
recompute the buffers exactly as the model's own initialisation does and check them at
every load; all PRM results in this paper use repaired buffers, and the affected scores
were discarded (registered deviation).

\paragraph{Compute.} All experiments ran on single cloud GPUs (mostly RTX 4090; some
follow-up runs on L40S and other GPUs of at least 24~GB, with BF16 and quantized runs of
a verifier always on the same GPU). The total cost of all experiments was about
55~EUR.

\section{Blinded audits of the outcome measure}\label{app:audit}

\paragraph{Format audit.} Fifty untruncated candidates without an extractable answer,
stratified by domain and lenient-grader verdict, were graded blind to generator,
verifier, condition and grader verdict by an AI model. The lenient grader accepted no
wrong answer (30/30 confirmed) and missed 5 of 20 correct ones; it is a conservative
lower bound on prose correctness.

\paragraph{Exhaustive c2 audit.} All 94 unique Best-of-32 winners without an extractable
final answer in either arm of the registered memory-frontier contrast, plus 30 random
winners with one, were shown to a human rater (the author) with the problem, the
reference answer and the full response, rendered with mathematical typesetting, and
nothing else; responses that reached the length limit were marked as such and graded as
incorrect by a rule fixed beforehand. The rater judged 83 correct, 29 incorrect and 12
unsure. A blinded second pass (same view, key unopened) re-examined every unsure and
incorrect verdict, resolved all 12 unsure verdicts as incorrect (9 with wrong reasoning,
3 with correct reasoning cut by the length limit) and confirmed every incorrect one; it
also flagged two GSM8K problems with questionable labels, both of which GSM8K-Platinum
removes. On the 30 controls the strict grader and the human agree on 29. On the 94
winners without an extractable answer the strict grader scores all as wrong, the human
judges 60 correct, and the lenient grader agrees with the human on 85.

\section{Analysis plans and deviations}\label{app:deviations}

The main study's plan registered five contrasts before any evaluation score was opened,
and an amendment, made after candidate pools existed but before any verifier had scored
them, renamed two hypotheses to match their estimands, fixed populations and test rules,
and set a decision rule for the calibration study (c3, not run). The two follow-up
studies were each fixed, with their item manifests and analysis code, before their
candidates were generated, and their pool hashes were recorded before scoring. The deviations,
all documented with dates in the repository, were: the Llama prompt repetition; the PRM
rotary-buffer repair and rescoring; the frozen calibration hyper-parameter of the unrun
c3 method; exploratory additions after the main results (bit-width dose-response, the
1.7B packed model in the frontier); in H6, the loss and regeneration of the first
candidate pool (never inspected) and a scoring split across four GPUs; in H7, the use of
GPUs other than the RTX 4090 for lack of availability and the computation of
lenient-grader verdicts with time limits equivalent to those on Linux; and corrections
to the follow-up analysis script made before H7 data existed, which reproduce all H6
results exactly.
