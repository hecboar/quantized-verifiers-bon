\begin{abstract}
Reward-model verifiers improve Best-of-$N$ answer selection but compete with the
generator for accelerator memory. We evaluate post-training weight quantization of
verifiers on frozen pools of 32 solutions per problem from GSM8K and MATH, using three
outcome reward models (1.7B--8B) from one family and a 7B process reward model (PRM),
with GPTQ, AWQ, NF4 and round-to-nearest. Analysis plans were fixed in version control
before verifier scoring, and for two follow-up studies before candidate generation.
On its prespecified 1,200-problem population, the 8B GPTQ-W4 contrast meets a
$\pm3$-point equivalence bound for Best-of-32 accuracy (difference $+0.33$; 90\%
interval $-0.58$ to $+1.25$). Two follow-up studies on unused MATH problems support
GPTQ-W4 equivalence for the 8B outcome verifier and the PRM. Where both verifiers beat
majority vote, the PRM retains an estimated 93\% of its 8.2-point advantage and passes
the prespecified half-retention test; with a stronger generator, neither verifier
clearly beat it. Two Llama-based reward models also stay within it
(losses 0.6--1.5 points). Under a verifier-only 6~GiB budget, a packed 4-bit 4B
outcome verifier outperforms the tested 1.7B full-precision verifier by 2.25 points
under strict grading and 1.40 points after a human audit, with similar measured
scoring throughput. Four-bit quantization often changes the selected candidate but much
less often its answer; 3-bit GPTQ with generic calibration degrades selection, and for
the Qwen-based verifiers in-domain calibration recovers most of the loss. Strict answer
grading can turn reward-model format preferences into spurious quantization gains, so verifier compression should be evaluated with
format-robust grading and against majority vote.
\end{abstract}
