\begin{abstract}
Reward-model verifiers improve Best-of-$N$ answer selection but compete with the
generator for accelerator memory. We evaluate post-training weight quantization of the
verifier on frozen pools of 32 solutions per problem from GSM8K and MATH, using three
outcome reward models (1.7B--8B) from one family and a 7B process reward model (PRM),
with GPTQ, AWQ, NF4 and round-to-nearest. Analysis plans were fixed in version control
before any verifier scoring, and those of two follow-up studies before their candidates
were generated. On its prespecified 1,200-problem population, the 8B GPTQ-W4 contrast
meets a $\pm3$-point equivalence bound for Best-of-32 accuracy (difference $+0.33$; 90\%
interval $-0.58$ to $+1.25$). Two follow-up studies on previously unused MATH problems
also support GPTQ-W4 equivalence for an 8B outcome verifier and the PRM. In the
weak-generator follow-up, where both verifiers beat majority vote, the PRM retains an
estimated 93\% of its 8.2-point advantage and passes the prespecified half-retention
test; with a stronger generator, no clear verifier advantage over majority vote was
established. Under a verifier-only 6~GB budget and fixed workload, a packed 4-bit 4B
outcome verifier outperforms the tested 1.7B full-precision verifier by 2.25 points
under strict grading and 1.40 points after a human audit of unparseable winners.
Four-bit quantization changes the selected candidate on a quarter to a half of problems
while the selected answer and aggregate accuracy change little, whereas 3-bit GPTQ can
degrade selection substantially. A strict answer grader can turn reward-model format
preferences into spurious quantization gains, so verifier compression should be
evaluated with format-robust grading and against majority vote.
\end{abstract}
